# The ear: Apple SpeechTranscriber vs WhisperKit, measured 2026-08-17

The founder dictated three messages through the strip that evening and asked
"find the issues". Six of the eight were the recognizer, not the tidy pass
("whisper bar" for Wispr, "is 2 bullets" for "lists to bullets", "Tairi pass"
for "tidy pass", "writing" for "riding", "No" for "Now", an inserted "I").
So the question this answers: is a stronger on-device ear available, what does
it cost, and what does it buy on THIS Mac (MacBook Air M3, 16 GB, macOS 26)?

Tooling: `brew install whisperkit-cli` (argmax OSS 1.1.0), Core ML models
into `~/Documents/huggingface` (large-v3_turbo is ~1.6 GB). Apple side: the
corpus's own `transcribe <file> en_US` (SpeechTranscriber, no vocabulary
layer) and, for the captured set, the app's actual output. Scorer:
word error rate after lowercasing, dropping punctuation and `$`, and treating
`3:15`, `2.5`, emails and paths as words (`scratchpad/ear/score.py`, kept
beside the reports for the session).

## Accuracy

| set | what it is | Apple | Whisper large-v3-turbo | small.en | medium.en |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C, 30 torture sentences (numbers, negations, names, paths) | Apple = app output | **12.8%** | **8.2%** | 16.8% | 22.4% |
| E, 30 sentences dense with names (Kizu, Gangothri, Wispr, Supabase…) | Apple = raw engine, no vocabulary layer | 35.5% | 31.2% | | |
| D, 30 Telugu/Hindi + English code-switched | | 50.6% | 93.3%: forced to English it TRANSLATED ("Tell Ravi that the meeting is postponed") | | |

Set C, where turbo wins: `3:15, not 3:50` (Apple "315, not 350" plus a
phantom "Eating,"), `40 attendees` (Apple "4 tea attendees"), `Reply to Aidan`
(Apple "Reply to reply to"), the API key (Apple "The the ABI key"). Where
Apple wins: `$120` (turbo "120 dollars"), `/Users/chetan/projects` (turbo
spelled out "slash users slash").

## The founder's own voice: 20 real dictations, side by side

Whisper turbo got right what Apple got wrong, on content that matters:
Kizu (Apple: Kiesel, Kazu), "Excel and stuff" (Apple: "excellent stuff"),
Gangotri (Apple: "Gango 3"), "no red flags" (Apple: "without refraction"),
"hookah" (Apple: "okay"), McClintock, "tidy pass" (Apple: "Tairi pass"),
"Priya is away" (Apple: "Korea is away"), and the spoken list came back as
"Second, we ship the draft" where Apple wrote "2nd. We shifted the draft".
Whisper's own faults: two clips came back all lower-case with no
punctuation, one hallucinated a leading "Baddie wannabe.", one starved-mic
clip degenerated to "the-- - - - -", and it keeps "you know" (the
deterministic pass removes that anyway).

Reading it plainly: **on this voice, turbo is the better ear.** Not
flawless, but the class of error the founder complained about is mostly gone.

## Latency, which is the whole problem

Per utterance, median over the 90 corpus clips (~9 s of audio each), after
the audio ends:

| model | encode | full pipeline | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple SpeechTranscriber | streams during speech | **~0.1 s at release** | what ships |
| Whisper large-v3-turbo | 1.21 s | **3.4 s** (p90 4.2 s) | the accurate one |
| Whisper medium.en | 0.35 s | 0.71 s | less accurate than Apple |
| Whisper small.en | 0.05 s | 0.17 s | less accurate than Apple |

Whisper encodes a fixed 30-second window; the accurate model costs over a
second just to encode on this Mac and two more to decode. Nothing here gets a
Whisper-quality result at release in under three seconds. WhisperKit does
stream (re-encoding as audio arrives), which would move most of that cost
under the hold, at a steady CPU/ANE load while talking, and still leave the
tail to decode at release. Not measured tonight.

## What this means

1. Apple stays the ear for the words that land instantly. Nothing else is
   fast enough for that job.
2. The only honest way to get turbo's accuracy is the shape the tidy pass
   already has: **a draft that lands at once, and a better version that
   replaces it in place a few seconds later.** The swap machinery from
   "instant, then tidied in place" would carry it: run Whisper turbo on the
   utterance's audio in the background, tidy ITS text, and swap when the
   policy allows. It would need the 4 s ceiling raised to ~6 s for long
   utterances, the same "not if you typed since" rule, and the same no-swap
   apps. The swap would visibly change words, not only fillers, which is
   exactly what the founder wants changed.
3. Costs to say out loud before choosing that: a 1.6 GB model download on
   first run, ~1.5 GB of memory while loaded, several seconds of ANE/CPU per
   utterance (battery), and the "never leaves the Mac" promise stays true.
4. Vocabulary matters more than model for names (set E: both engines over 30%
   raw); the learned-names layer applies to either engine's output.

Not built. This is the answer to the spike; the founder decides.
